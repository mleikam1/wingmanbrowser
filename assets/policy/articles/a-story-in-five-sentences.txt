Write a tiny fictional story using five sentences. In the first, introduce a character and a place. In the second, give the character something they want. In the third, add an unexpected obstacle. In the fourth, let the character choose what to do. In the fifth, show what changed.

For example, your character might be a gardener searching for a misplaced notebook. The obstacle could be a sudden shower. The choice could be to ask a neighbor for help. You decide whether finding the notebook is the real ending or whether the new friendship matters more.

Read your draft aloud if you feel comfortable. Replace one vague word with a concrete detail. Then change the final sentence and notice how it changes the whole story.

This is an original Wingman writing exercise. The Library of Congress is a reference for studying source material; the fictional example above is not a historical account.
