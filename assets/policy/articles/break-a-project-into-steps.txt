Write one sentence describing what you need to finish. A poster about water is a broad goal. A poster with a title, a diagram, three facts, and source notes is easier to plan.

List the pieces you need. Put them in an order that respects dependencies: understand the topic before choosing your three facts, then sketch the layout before making the final version. Leave room to check the work.

Choose a next action small enough to begin now. It might be drawing three empty boxes for a diagram or writing one question to ask your teacher. Mark completed steps and update the plan when you learn something new.

A plan is a tool you can revise. If the instructions are unclear or you need an accommodation, ask the person who assigned the work. This is an original Wingman planning prompt; the reference is a source-study resource for research projects.
