Geologists group rocks by how they formed. Igneous rocks formed as molten material cooled. Sedimentary rocks formed from accumulated material or substances deposited from water. Metamorphic rocks changed from earlier rocks under conditions such as heat and pressure.

A single color is rarely enough to identify a rock. Grain size, layers, texture, and the place it came from provide more clues. Some changes happen over times far longer than a human life.

Start with an ordinary rock you already have permission to handle. Draw it without guessing its name. Record visible details: smooth or rough, large or tiny grains, bands or no bands. Keep observations separate from your interpretation.

Do not break rocks or collect them from protected places for this exercise. A photograph, a museum specimen, or a stone left in place can be enough. A good field note also says when you are unsure.
