The Moon does not make its own visible light. We see sunlight reflected from its surface. As the Moon travels around Earth, our view of its sunlit half changes. Those changing views are its phases.

A crescent shows a narrow part of the bright half. A quarter Moon looks half lit from Earth; the name describes its place in the cycle. At full Moon, the side facing us is nearly fully illuminated. Ordinary phases are not caused by Earth's shadow. A lunar eclipse is a separate event.

Make a Moon journal using only your eyes. From a safe place, sketch the shape when the Moon is visible. Add the date, approximate time, and direction. Leave a blank for cloudy days instead of inventing an observation. After several weeks, compare your drawings.

Your journal is a record of what you saw, not a test you must get right. Never look at the Sun through binoculars or a telescope.
