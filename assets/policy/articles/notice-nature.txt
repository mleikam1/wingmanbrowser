Choose a familiar outdoor place where you are allowed to spend time. A garden, courtyard, or view from a window can work. Draw a rough map and mark one plant, one sound, and one patch of light. Repeat the observation another day.

Record what you can actually observe. Instead of writing that a bird is happy, describe its movements or calls. Instead of naming an unfamiliar plant confidently, describe its leaf shape. Questions are useful entries too.

Keep the place welcoming for its other visitors and living things. Follow local rules, leave natural objects where they are, give wildlife space, and take your litter away. Stay on appropriate paths and avoid disturbing plants to improve a photograph.

Compare your pages at the end of a week. What changed? What remained? This is an original Wingman observation activity informed by the National Park Service's outdoor stewardship guidance.
