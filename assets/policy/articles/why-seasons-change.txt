Earth's rotation axis is tilted. As Earth travels around the Sun, that tilt changes how directly sunlight reaches each hemisphere and how long its days are. The Northern and Southern Hemispheres therefore have opposite seasons.

Distance from the Sun is not the main cause of the familiar seasonal pattern. The Northern Hemisphere is actually having winter when Earth is closest to the Sun. The important starting idea is the angle and duration of sunlight.

Try a drawing: make two Earth circles on opposite sides of a Sun. Draw their axes leaning in the same direction on your paper. Label north and south. Decide which hemisphere tilts toward the Sun in each position.

Then separate climate from a single day's weather. A cool summer day does not erase the seasonal pattern. Write one question your drawing helps answer and one it does not, such as why a particular city gets heavy rain.
