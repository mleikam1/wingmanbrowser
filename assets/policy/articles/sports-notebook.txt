A sports notebook

A sports notebook can help you notice more than the final result. Choose a sport because it interests you, then write a question you can explore: how do teammates communicate, when does a team change its approach, or what makes a rule difficult to understand?

Separate three kinds of note. An observation describes what you saw. A question marks something you do not yet know. An interpretation offers a possible explanation. “Two teammates pointed toward open space” is an observation; “they planned the next pass” is an interpretation unless you have evidence for it.

Record the date and source when you consult an official rules resource. Different age groups, competitions, or seasons can use different rules. A heading that looks familiar is not enough to establish which version applies. Keep unresolved questions in the notebook instead of inventing an answer.

The Olympic Studies Centre names excellence, respect, and friendship among the movement's values. You can use these as prompts for your own reflection: what showed thoughtful preparation, consideration for others, or cooperation? These prompts are Wingman's original activity, not an official Olympic lesson or an endorsement.

End each entry with one detail worth remembering and one question to explore later. You may record a result you personally verified, but this notebook supplies no schedules, scores, live feed, predictions, or betting information. Its purpose is attentive observation, not ranking your productivity or judging other people.
