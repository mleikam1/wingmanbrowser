The ocean does not stay at one height. At a shoreline, its surface rises toward high tide and falls toward low tide. The height difference is the tidal range. Tides are long waves driven by the gravitational influence of the Moon and Sun.

The tide you observe belongs to a particular place. A drawing of two ocean bulges is a useful starting model, but it is not a local tide timetable. Coastlines and the shape of ocean basins affect what happens at a beach.

Try a paper observation: sketch a familiar shoreline at two imaginary water levels. Mark which rocks would be covered, where boats could float, and which habitats change as water moves. Write down what your sketch cannot tell you, such as the actual time of high tide.

If you visit a coast, use current local safety information and follow posted guidance. This offline introduction is not a navigation or tide forecast.
